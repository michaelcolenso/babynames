## Joseph's ninety-five years

Joseph is the undisputed champion of the tableland. It crossed half its peak in {{claim:longestStart}} and did not dip below that threshold until {{claim:longestEnd}} — an unbroken run of {{claim:longestYears}} consecutive years.

At its peak in {{claim:longestPeakYear}}, Joseph recorded {{claim:longestPeak}} boys. That was at the crest of the postwar baby boom. But Joseph did not need a baby boom to survive. It crossed World War I, the Great Depression, World War II, the sixties counterculture, and the turn of the millennium without ever losing half its audience.

A boy named Joseph in 1914 could have held a great-grandson named Joseph in 2008, and the name would have held the exact same tier on both birth certificates.

## A seventy percent male fortress

The tableland divides sharply along gender lines.

Boys were named for grandfathers, saints, and lineage. Parents treated a son's name as an heirloom, renewed across generations without revision. Girls were subject to fashion. Female names surged and retreated in generational tides, leaving only a small handful of female tablelands.

## The 1971 fracture

Look closely at the timeline and an unexpected structural pattern appears.

Many of the greatest male plateaus — [James](/name/James/) ({{claim:jamesYears}} years), [John](/name/John/) ({{claim:johnYears}} years), and [William](/name/William/) ({{claim:williamYears}} years) — ended in the exact same year: 1971.

James crested at {{claim:jamesPeak}} boys in {{claim:jamesPeakYear}} and sustained its plateau from {{claim:jamesStart}} to {{claim:jamesEnd}}. When the baby boom receded in the late 1960s, the traditional naming compact finally cracked.

Only a few names managed to build modern plateaus after the break. [Daniel](/name/Daniel/) climbed to a peak of {{claim:danielPeak}} in {{claim:danielPeakYear}} and maintained its plateau for {{claim:danielYears}} years, stretching from {{claim:danielStart}} to {{claim:danielEnd}}.
