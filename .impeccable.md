# .impeccable.md

Design context for Name Vitals — consumed by all frontend-design skills.

## Design Context

### Users

Two overlapping audiences: **expectant parents** researching whether a name is trending, dying out, or timeless; and **name enthusiasts / data nerds** who find the cultural history and trend data intrinsically interesting. Both deserve a design that treats the data seriously without condescension. The parent is shopping; the enthusiast is exploring. The interface serves both without compromise.

### Brand Personality

**Authoritative. Curious. Refined.**

Name Vitals is the publication of record for baby name history. It doesn't shout. It presents. It lets the data do the talking with quiet confidence. Think: a well-edited longform piece about cultural trends, typeset carefully, with charts that earn their place.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Editorial / magazine** — typographically precise, data-forward, serious without being sterile.

The existing foundation is correct: warm off-white (`#f7f5f2`), Iowan Old Style serif, subtle structure. Build on it rather than replace it.

**References to draw from:**

- Are.na: restrained, typographically intentional, lets content breathe
- The Pudding: data journalism that feels crafted, not generated
- Craig Mod's writing/design: serif-led, warm, precise, respects the reader's intelligence

**Anti-references (explicitly avoid):**

- BabyCenter / What to Expect: pastel overload, stock photography, commercial register, feels like it's selling something
- Generic SaaS dashboard: purple-to-blue gradients, card grids, hero metrics, AI aesthetic fingerprints
- Wikipedia: technically correct but personality-free, pure utility with no craft

### Design Principles

1. **Let the data speak.** Charts and numbers are primary content, not decorations. Typography and layout serve the data, not the other way around.
2. **Earn every element.** Nothing decorative that doesn't reinforce meaning. Status colors communicate, sparklines inform, whitespace creates hierarchy.
3. **Refined, not precious.** Serif typography and warm tones signal care — not luxury. This is a research tool with craft, not a premium product.
4. **Both audiences, one interface.** Don't design for either extreme. The parent should find what they need without a data-science degree; the enthusiast shouldn't feel talked down to.
5. **Light and dark, properly done.** Dark mode is not an afterthought or a color-inverted light mode. It gets its own considered palette — ink-on-paper / paper-in-lamplight duality.
