# Debut of the Year

Some names don't fade in. They arrive — from nothing, at scale, in a single year — because something on a screen or a stage or a ballot put them there. If you take every name and ask which one *debuted* with the biggest splash in each year, you don't get a list of names. You get a timeline of what America was watching.

Below is that almanac. For every year since 1910, the boy's and girl's name that appeared for the first time with the largest single-year count — the loudest debut. Flip between Boys and Girls, or filter to the ones with a clear cultural trigger. Each name is sized by how big its arrival was, and links to its full history.

## One event named a whole generation

Read top to bottom, the eras change costume. The early decades belong to **war heroes and headlines** — **Foch** (1918) for the marshal who won the First World War, **Mcarther** for the general, **Willkie** for a presidential nominee, **Rockne** for a coach killed in a plane crash. Then come the **TV cowboys** — **Maverick**, **Rowdy**, **Hoby** — followed by the **idols** (**Illya** of *The Man from U.N.C.L.E.*) and the **martyrs** (**Medgar**, in the year Medgar Evers was killed).

But the single loudest moment in the whole record belongs to one television event. In 1977, *Roots* aired, and it produced the biggest debut of **both** sexes: **Levar** (523 boys, for LeVar Burton) and **Kizzy** (1,117 girls, for Kunta Kinte's daughter). No other event in a century comes close — Kizzy is the largest single-year name debut on record, period.

## The girls' side is louder — and more televised

After about 1970 the debut engine runs almost entirely on Black and Latino popular culture, and the girls' list is the more media-saturated of the two. TV heroines: **Cinnamon**, **Fallon**, **Jaleesa**, **Moesha**, **Vanellope**, **Rey**. R&B and hip-hop: **Sade**, **Erykah**, **Kelis**, **Kehlani**, **Zhavia**, **Pharrell**, **Jahseh**. And a deep telenovela current that the boys' side only hints at — **Isamar**, **Yamilex**, **Greidys**, **Dalary**. The boys lean a little more toward sports and rap; the girls toward television and divas. Both converge on the same truth: for fifty years, the names that arrive loudest have been the ones America's screens taught it to love.

## A glitch crashes the party

Two years are won not by a person but by a bug. In 1989 the biggest "debut" for boys is **Christop** and for girls **Alexandr** — Christopher and Alexandra with their final letters chopped off, a one-year data error in which a batch of names was truncated to eight characters. They're flagged in red above. We left them in on purpose: a good almanac shows its seams, and the data's own mistakes are part of its history too.
