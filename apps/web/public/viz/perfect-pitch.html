35 / THE CHORUS

Perfect Pitch

Every year of American birth records, played as ten-note chords. In 1880, one name — Mary — rings out over everything else, a single dominant voice. By today that voice has dissolved into a wide, quiet shimmer of hundreds of names, no one of them loud enough to hear alone. Press play and listen to a monoculture become a chorus.

How to listen

Each year plays as a quick rolled chord: the #1 girl and #1 boy name sound first, low and loud, followed by ranks 2 through 10 rippling upward into a higher, quieter shimmer. A note's volume is exactly its share of that year's top-10 births — the same math behind the Concentration Index. A low drone hums underneath the whole piece; its filter opens up (brighter, more resonant) in years when one name dominated, and closes down (darker, more muffled) as the naming pool fragmented. A small chime marks every decade, and a brighter ding — with a triangle on the timeline — marks the moment the #1 name changes hands.

Scrub anywhere on the timeline to jump straight to a decade. Isolate just the girls' or boys' line with the Voices control. The whole run is about a minute at 1× speed, or under 15 seconds at 8× if you just want the shape of the century.

Built on public-domain SSA data. Note gains, HHI drone modulation and rank-1 turnover are all computed from the same precomputed /api/meta and /api/concentration payloads that back the rest of the site.
