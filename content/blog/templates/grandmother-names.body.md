## The rule

The bar was a hundred thousand living bearers and a population that is ninety percent fifty-plus. {{claim:count}} names cleared it, out of the {{claim:atFloor}} names big enough to be considered.

Living counts are estimates: birth records aged forward with a life table. They also carry one blind spot. The Social Security Administration lists a name only in the years at least five babies get it.

Every one is a girls' name. {{claim:femaleCount}} of {{claim:count}}. Zero boys' names made the list.

That is not because boys' names never get old. Lower the floor to twenty-five thousand living bearers and {{claim:maleNearMissCount}} male names qualify: {{claim:maleNearMissNames}}. Every one is a short form. The long versions behind them — James, William, Thomas, Robert — are still common on new birth certificates. Jim, Bill, Tom, and Bob are not.

The average name on it has {{claim:avgLiving}} living bearers and a median age of {{claim:avgMedianAge}}. Across the whole group, {{claim:avgOver50Pct}} percent of the people who answer to these names are fifty or older.

Susan and Mateo are the same kind of fact. Susan is what America was naming girls when the country was young. Mateo is what it is naming boys now. The record does not rank them. It only counts who got the name, and when.
