# The Plateaus — American Names That Refused to Budge

In 1914, American parents put Joseph on 18829 birth certificates. In 2008, ninety-four years later, they were still putting it on 16599.

Some names spike and collapse inside a decade. Others build and recede over a half-century. But a distinct group did something rarer: they reached a high tableland and refused to leave.

We measured every name that peaked above 5,000 births and stayed at or above half its peak count for thirty consecutive years.

107 names qualified. The average stay lasted 42 years.

## Joseph's ninety-five years

Joseph is the undisputed champion of the tableland. It crossed half its peak in 1914 and did not dip below that threshold until 2008 — an unbroken run of 95 consecutive years.

At its peak in 1956, Joseph recorded 32749 boys. That was at the crest of the postwar baby boom. But Joseph did not need a baby boom to survive. It crossed World War I, the Great Depression, World War II, the sixties counterculture, and the turn of the millennium without ever losing half its audience.

A boy named Joseph in 1914 could have held a great-grandson named Joseph in 2008, and the name would have held the exact same tier on both birth certificates.

## A seventy percent male fortress

The tableland divides sharply along gender lines.

75 of the 107 plateaus belong to boys — a 70 percent majority. Only 32 belong to girls.

The average boy on this list stayed at the summit for 44 years. The average girl lasted 39 years.

Boys were named for grandfathers, saints, and lineage. Parents treated a son's name as an heirloom, renewed across generations without revision. Girls were subject to fashion. Female names surged and retreated in generational tides, leaving only a small handful of female tablelands.

## The women of the tableland

Elizabeth holds the female record. Between 1946 and 2009, Elizabeth spent 64 consecutive years at or above half its 1990 peak of 20750 girls.

Maria held its ground for 59 years, from 1949 to 2007. Ann held for 57 years, and Rebecca for 50 years.

Then there is Mary. Mary reached 73984 births in 1921 and spent 51 straight years on the plateau. In an elementary school classroom in 1950, you did not ask if there was a Mary. You asked which desk she sat in.

## The 1971 fracture

Look closely at the timeline and an unexpected structural pattern appears.

Many of the greatest male plateaus — [James](/name/James/) (52 years), [John](/name/John/) (57 years), and [William](/name/William/) (57 years) — ended in the exact same year: 1971.

James crested at 94767 boys in 1947 and sustained its plateau from 1920 to 1971. When the baby boom receded in the late 1960s, the traditional naming compact finally cracked.

Only a few names managed to build modern plateaus after the break. [Daniel](/name/Daniel/) climbed to a peak of 38559 in 1985 and maintained its plateau for 57 years, stretching from 1951 to 2007.
